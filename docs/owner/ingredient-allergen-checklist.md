# Ingredients & allergens checklist — for the owner

**Private working document. Not shown on the website.**
Prepared 2 October 2026 to help you collect the real information for each product.

## Why this matters

Before anyone can buy a food online in the UK, they must be able to see its full
ingredients and allergens. That information has to be true for **your** product,
so it must come from **your own recipes, or your suppliers' labels and spec sheets**.

The "typical ingredients" below are only a starting point, so you know what to
look for. They are **not** facts about your products. Recipes for these snacks vary
a lot (for example, some chin chin has egg and some doesn't; some kokoro is fried
in groundnut oil), so please check every line against the real thing.

## The 14 allergens UK law requires you to declare

1. Celery
2. Cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats)
3. Crustaceans (e.g. prawns, crab)
4. Eggs
5. Fish
6. Lupin
7. Milk
8. Molluscs (e.g. mussels, squid)
9. Mustard
10. Peanuts (groundnuts)
11. Sesame
12. Soya
13. Sulphur dioxide / sulphites (above 10mg/kg — sometimes in dried fruit, some sugars and preservatives)
14. Tree nuts (almonds, hazelnuts, walnuts, cashews, pecans, Brazil nuts, pistachios, macadamias)

Coconut is **not** one of the 14 in the UK. It's still worth stating clearly,
because some people avoid it.

## Questions that apply to every product

- **Who makes it?** Do you make it yourself, or buy it in from a supplier?
  - **Bought in, already packed:** copy the ingredients and allergens from the supplier's label.
  - **Made by you, packed by you, sold online:** you need your own full ingredients
    list with allergens. If you also pack it for sale on your premises, check the
    rules for "prepacked for direct sale" (Natasha's Law).
- **Which frying oil?** Groundnut (peanut) oil must be declared as **peanuts**.
  Ask about soya oil and other blends too.
- **May contain:** is it made in a kitchen or factory that also handles peanuts,
  tree nuts, sesame, gluten, milk or egg? If so, you may need a "may contain"
  warning.
- **Storage:** how should customers store it, and how long does it keep once
  opened?

For official guidance, see the Food Standards Agency website (food.gov.uk), under
business guidance on allergens.
