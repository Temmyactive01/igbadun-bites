# Igbadun Bites — Style Guide

## Brand tone
Warm, nostalgic, celebratory. "Bringing Back Memories, One Bite at a Time." This is
about reconnecting with home, sharing with family, joy — not a generic snack-shop
tone. Copy should feel personal, never corporate.

## Color direction (from brand brief: brown, gold, green, cream)
Until the business owner sends exact brand hex codes, use this palette as the
working default — warm and food-appropriate, avoids looking like a generic
bootstrap template:

- **Cream / background:** `#FAF3E7` (warm off-white, not stark white)
- **Deep brown (primary text / headers):** `#3B2416`
- **Gold (accent, CTAs, highlights):** `#C9972D`
- **Forest green (secondary accent):** `#2F4A32`
- **Terracotta (optional warm accent for sale tags, badges):** `#B5542A`

Avoid pure white backgrounds and pure black text — keep everything warm. Avoid
generic "e-commerce blue" anywhere.

## Typography
- Headings: a warm serif or slab-serif (e.g. "Fraunces," "Lora," or similar) —
  gives a handmade, heritage feel appropriate to a food heritage brand.
- Body: a clean, highly legible sans-serif (e.g. "Inter" or "Work Sans") for
  product details, prices, forms.
- Avoid default system fonts — this is a visual-identity-forward brand, not a
  utility app.

## Visual approach (no real photos yet)
Until real product photography arrives:
- Use elegant placeholder cards with the product name in the heading font, a
  colored background block (rotate through the palette), and a simple line-art
  or icon motif — NOT a generic gray "image placeholder" box.
- Subtle African-pattern-inspired border or divider details are welcome (geometric,
  not literal/stereotyped imagery) — tasteful, not kitsch.
- Generous whitespace, warm shadows (brown-tinted, not pure gray), rounded but not
  overly soft corners.

## What "good" looks like here
- Feels like a real, loved small food brand — not a SaaS dashboard wearing
  brown and gold.
- Product cards should feel appetizing even without real photos — typography and
  color carry it.
- Mobile-first: most customers will browse and order from their phone.
- Every interactive element (Add to cart, Checkout, Sign in) should feel
  deliberate — smooth transitions (150–250ms), clear hover/press states.

## What to avoid
- Default shadcn/Tailwind "starter template" look with no customization.
- Stock e-commerce clichés (generic cart icon badges, default blue links).
- Overly literal "African print" clipart — this brand is about authenticity and
  warmth, not costume-y decoration.
